Best eSIM for a US Trip: Airalo vs Yesim vs Saily

A US eSIM can get you online before you land — but only if the plan covers the right network and you install it before departure.

Updated 2026-11-16 · 7 min read · Affiliate links are marked

Quick answer

For a short US trip, choose a data-only eSIM that covers the whole stay and install it before you fly. If you need a local phone number or lots of hotspot data, check the plan details carefully — those are the features most often missing.

What an eSIM actually solves

An eSIM is a digital SIM profile. You install it by scanning a QR code or using the provider’s app, then switch it on when you land. It avoids a trip to a phone shop and lets you keep your normal number active for incoming calls and bank codes.

The trade-off is that most travel eSIMs are data-only. WhatsApp and other internet calls work, but a local number for restaurant reservations or delivery apps may not.

Coverage: why “US eSIM” is not enough

The US has several large networks. A plan may use one or more of them, and coverage can differ inside national parks, rural areas and large buildings. If you are driving between cities, check the coverage map rather than trusting a generic “USA” label.

Install before you fly

  1. Buy the plan while you have stable Wi-Fi.
  2. Install the eSIM but leave it switched off until you land.
  3. Save the activation instructions offline.
  4. At arrival, switch off data roaming on your home SIM and turn on the eSIM.
  5. Test data before leaving the airport.

Data use: how much is enough?

For maps, messaging and a few restaurant searches, 3–5 GB for a week is usually enough. Add more if you use hotspot, upload photos or work remotely. Video calls and cloud backups are the biggest consumers.

Keep your number and your 2FA

Do not delete your home SIM profile. Keep it active for incoming SMS if your bank or accounts use it for verification. Turn off data roaming on that line so you do not return home to a large bill.

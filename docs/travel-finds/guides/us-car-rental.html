US Car Rental for Visitors: 9 Fees That Change the Total

The daily rate is only the beginning. In the US, insurance, airport fees, tolls and fuel can add more than the car itself — especially for visitors.

Updated 2026-11-16 · 8 min read · Affiliate links are marked

Quick answer

Compare the total at pickup, not the daily rate: base fare + taxes + airport fee + insurance + tolls + fuel + young-driver fee. If the total is within 10–15% of the cheapest quote, choose the simpler pickup and clearer insurance terms.

1. Start with the total, not the headline price

US rental search results often exclude several charges. Before you book, open the “total for stay” view and check what is included. A $39/day car can easily become $75–95/day after airport fees, taxes and insurance.

  • Base rate: the number you see first.
  • Taxes and airport concession fees: normal at US airports and often 10–20%.
  • Collision damage waiver or excess insurance: optional, but can be expensive at the counter.
  • Liability insurance: may be required; check what your home policy or credit card already covers.
  • Under-25 fee: usually charged per day until age 25.
  • Additional driver: often free for a spouse, but not always.
  • Tolls: cashless roads may charge a daily fee or a large admin fee.
  • Fuel: “full-to-full” is usually cheaper than prepaid fuel if you drive normally.
  • One-way fee: returning to a different city can cost more than the rental itself.

2. Check your existing insurance before buying at the counter

Many US credit cards include collision damage coverage for rentals, but the rules depend on the card, the country and the vehicle type. Check the exclusions before you travel. Liability is different: it covers damage you cause to other people or property and may not be included by default.

If you buy cover at the counter, ask for the total price and what is still excluded. Do not accept a vague “full coverage” answer. The important question is: what is the excess and what does the policy not pay?

3. Tolls, parking and city fees

Parts of the US are cashless: tolls are photographed and charged later. Rental companies may add a daily convenience fee plus an admin fee. If you will use toll roads, compare the rental company’s toll package with a personal transponder or a pay-by-plate option.

Parking is often the hidden cost of a city trip. In San Francisco, New York, Chicago, Boston or Los Angeles, hotel parking can cost more than the car. Check parking before choosing a hotel outside the centre.

4. Licence, credit card and deposit

Bring the licence you will actually use, plus an International Driving Permit if your licence is not in English. A credit card in the main driver’s name is usually required for the deposit; debit cards are not accepted everywhere. The deposit can be several hundred dollars and is blocked, not charged, if everything goes smoothly.

5. How to compare the three main options

Use one search for the same dates, pickup point and car class. Then compare the total including insurance and fees. The offer cards below go to partners that can show US pickup options; use them as starting points, not as a guarantee of the lowest price.

6. Pickup checklist

  1. Photograph the car and note existing damage before leaving the lot.
  2. Confirm the fuel policy and the return time.
  3. Ask where the toll transponder is and how billing works.
  4. Check the spare tyre, warning triangle and charging cable.
  5. Save the roadside assistance number in your phone.
